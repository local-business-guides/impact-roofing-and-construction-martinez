What should emergency hail repair cover?

Emergency work is meant to limit further damage and make the roof safer until lasting repairs can be completed. Depending on the damage, that may mean covering an exposed area, securing loose material, or addressing a leak that is getting into the home. The right response depends on what a roofer finds, not just how large the hail looked.

Ask the contractor to explain which work is temporary and which work is intended to last. A tarp or other temporary covering can help protect the home, but it is not a substitute for repairing damaged roofing. Get the proposed work and any limitations in writing.

What will a local roofer check after hail?

A careful inspection should look at the roof covering, flashing, vents, valleys, and other places where water can get through. The roofer should also check for loose or displaced materials, damaged gutters, and signs of water inside the attic or living space. Hail marks can be difficult to assess from the ground, so don’t rely on a quick look from the driveway.

Ask for clear photos or notes showing the areas of concern. If the roofer sees damage inside, that should be documented too. An inspection should distinguish hail-related damage from ordinary wear where possible, and explain what needs attention now versus what can wait.

What should you do while waiting for an inspection?

Protect people first. Don’t climb onto a wet, steep, or damaged roof, and keep away from sagging ceilings or areas with water near electrical fixtures. If water is entering the home, move belongings out of the way only when it’s safe to do so.

  • Take photos of visible damage from the ground and of any water inside, if it is safe.
  • Note when the hail and leak occurred, and which rooms or areas are affected.
  • Use a container to catch drips if you can do so without standing under a bulging ceiling.
  • Ask the roofer how they will protect the exposed area and when they can inspect it.
  • Keep receipts and records for any immediate steps you take to limit further damage.

Should you repair the roof or replace it?

A repair may be suitable when the damage is limited and the surrounding roof is in serviceable condition. More extensive damage, widespread deterioration, or materials that can’t be matched or properly repaired may lead a contractor to discuss replacement. Hail alone does not determine the answer.

Ask the roofer to show you the damage and explain why the proposed scope is appropriate. If replacement is recommended, ask what repair options were considered and what risks come with choosing a smaller repair. For an insurance claim, keep the contractor’s findings separate from the insurer’s coverage decision.

What affects the cost and timing?

The cost depends on the amount and location of damage, the roofing material, how easy the area is to reach, and whether the work is temporary or permanent. Damage to flashing, vents, decking, or interior areas can change the scope. The contractor should explain which items are necessary and identify any work that cannot be confirmed until damaged materials are opened.

Timing can depend on current demand after a storm, weather, material availability, roof access, and whether additional damage is found. Temporary protection and permanent repair may happen on different schedules. Ask what can be done first, what remains to be completed, and how the contractor will update you if the plan changes.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Can you inspect the roof and explain which damage needs attention right away?
  • What temporary protection do you recommend, and when can permanent repairs be completed?
  • Will I receive photos and a written description of the proposed work?
  • Which parts of the estimate are temporary, and which are permanent repairs?
  • What materials are included, and how will you handle any damage found during the repair?
  • Who will do the work, and who should I contact if I have questions afterward?
  • Do I need a permit for the proposed work, and who will confirm local requirements?
  • How do you coordinate your documentation with an insurance claim without guaranteeing coverage?

A clear answer should be specific to your roof, not a promise made before anyone has inspected it. Get the scope, expected timing, and any exclusions in writing.
