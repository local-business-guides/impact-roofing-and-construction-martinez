What does roof replacement include?

A replacement generally means removing some or all of the existing roofing, checking the surface beneath it, and installing a new roofing system. The written proposal should make clear what materials are included, which parts of the roof are being replaced, and how cleanup and disposal will be handled.

The work may also involve replacing damaged decking, updating flashing around roof openings and edges, or addressing ventilation details. Those items should be identified as included, excluded, or subject to an additional charge if hidden damage is found. Ask for a clear explanation rather than assuming that “new roof” means every roof component is new.

What should a local professional inspect?

An estimate should begin with a close look at the roof, not just a view from the driveway. The contractor should assess its size and shape, existing roofing layers, visible wear, roof edges, valleys, penetrations, flashing, ventilation, and access for the crew. If there are signs of leaks or soft spots, ask how those will be checked and documented.

The condition beneath the shingles or other covering may not be clear until removal begins. Ask how the company will handle damaged decking or other concealed problems, who approves added work, and how the price changes if repairs are needed. Photos and written notes can help you understand why a repair is proposed.

What drives roof replacement cost in Augusta?

The biggest factors are the roof's size, slope, layout, and the materials you select. A roof with several valleys, dormers, edges, or penetrations can take more labor and detailing than a simple layout. The existing roof's condition also matters, especially if it has multiple layers or damage that only becomes visible after removal.

  • Removal and disposal of the existing roof covering.
  • Roofing material, underlayment, flashing, and other specified components.
  • Repairs to decking or other concealed damage.
  • Roof access, pitch, and the amount of detailed work around edges and openings.
  • Ventilation changes, cleanup, and any permit or inspection requirements.

A quote should spell out these items and identify allowances or exclusions. If two estimates differ, compare their scopes line by line before judging the total. There is no responsible way to give one price for every Augusta home without knowing the roof and the proposed work.

Which roofing options should you compare?

Ask which materials are suitable for your roof and what each proposal includes, rather than comparing product names alone. The right choice depends on your home's design, the existing roof structure, your budget, and the product details the contractor recommends. Request the manufacturer and product information in writing, along with any stated warranty terms.

Also compare the installation details. Two estimates can name similar roofing materials but differ in underlayment, flashing, ventilation work, removal, or repair allowances. Ask the contractor to explain those differences in plain language before you choose.

What can change the project timeline?

Timing depends on the roof's size and complexity, crew availability, material delivery, weather, and whether concealed damage needs repair. Permit or inspection requirements can also affect the schedule, so ask who checks local requirements and whether that work is included in the plan.

Before work begins, get an expected start window, a reasonable estimate of how long installation will take, and a plan for communicating delays. Ask how the crew will protect the home if weather interrupts the job, and when the site should be clear of debris.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing on the total alone without checking whether each estimate covers the same work.
  • Assuming decking repairs, flashing, disposal, or ventilation are included when the proposal does not say so.
  • Accepting a verbal promise instead of getting the scope, payment terms, and warranty information in writing.
  • Skipping questions about who will supervise the job and who to contact if an issue appears.
  • Signing before you understand how concealed damage or other changes will be approved and priced.

Take time to read the agreement and keep a copy of the estimate, product details, and any approved changes. If an answer is unclear, ask for it in writing before work starts.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What parts of the roof are included in this estimate, and what is excluded?
  • Which materials and installation components are specified?
  • How will you document and price damaged decking or other hidden issues?
  • Who will manage the work, and who will be on site?
  • What are the expected start window and project duration?
  • Who handles permit or inspection requirements, if they apply?
  • What cleanup, disposal, and protection of the property are included?
  • What workmanship and manufacturer warranty terms will I receive in writing?

What's the next step?

Arrange an inspection and ask for a written, itemized proposal for your home. Compare the scope, materials, exclusions, schedule, and warranty details, then ask the contractor to explain any line you don't understand.
