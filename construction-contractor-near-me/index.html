Get clear on expectations

Before work begins, talk through what you want completed and what decisions are still open. Bring up any access issues, areas that need special attention, and the way you expect to use the finished space. For a remodeling project, think about which features are essential and which could be adjusted as the plan takes shape.

Ask the contractor to discuss how the work will be approached and what information is needed from you. A good project conversation should leave you with a clearer picture of the next steps. Prepare your home and project notes

You can make an initial conversation more useful with a little preparation:

  • Write a brief description of the work you want done.
  • Take photos of the rooms, roof, or exterior areas involved.
  • List any known problems, such as a door that sticks or a room that needs more usable space.
  • Gather examples of styles, materials, or layouts you like.
  • Think about household routines that could be affected during the project.

You don't have to settle every detail before asking for guidance. It's more helpful to share what you know and flag the parts you haven't decided yet.

Avoid rushed decisions

Don't assume that a contractor who handles one kind of project is automatically the right fit for another. A roof replacement, a bathroom renovation, and a home addition each call for different discussions. Also, avoid making choices based only on a photo or a short description. Explain your goals and ask how the proposed work relates to the conditions at your home.

It's sensible to call a professional when the job changes a room's layout, adds living space, involves roofing, or combines interior and exterior work.
