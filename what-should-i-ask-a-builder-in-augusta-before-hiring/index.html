What should a builder's work include?

A builder may coordinate several parts of a project, from planning and material ordering to trade work, inspections, and final cleanup. The exact responsibilities depend on the job and contract, so ask what the builder will do directly and what will be handled by subcontractors.

Before hiring, get a written scope that describes the work area, materials or finish allowances, what is excluded, and how you will approve changes. It should also explain payment milestones, who manages the site, and what happens if hidden damage or other unexpected work is found.

What should a local builder check before giving an estimate?

A useful estimate starts with a visit and a clear understanding of your plans. The builder should look at the existing structure and access, note visible damage or conditions that may affect the work, and ask about your priorities, materials, and timing.

For a remodel, that can include checking how the proposed work connects to existing walls, floors, plumbing, electrical, or roofing. Ask which details are confirmed and which are assumptions. If an area cannot be inspected until work begins, ask how the builder will document the condition and price any added work.

What changes the cost of a building project?

There is no single price that fits every project. Cost depends on the size and complexity of the work, the materials and finishes you choose, the condition of the existing structure, site access, and the number of trades or approvals involved.

Ask for a breakdown that separates labor, materials, allowances, and work that is not included. An allowance is a placeholder for an item that has not been selected or fully priced, so ask what happens if your final choice costs more or less. Compare bids line by line, not just by the total.

Hidden conditions can also affect the final cost. Ask how the builder will pause, show you the issue, and get written approval before proceeding with work that was not in the original scope.

What can affect the project schedule?

Timing depends on the project scope, the order and availability of materials, trade schedules, inspections, and how quickly selections and decisions are made. Work uncovered after demolition can also change the sequence or require additional steps.

Ask for a target start window, an estimated duration, and the main milestones rather than relying on a single promised finish date. Find out how often the builder will update you, how delays will be communicated, and whether you need to be available to approve choices or access the home.

What should you check about Augusta area permits and inspections?

Permit and inspection requirements depend on the work and the property's exact jurisdiction. Ask the builder which approvals may apply, who will confirm the requirements, who will submit applications, and whether inspections are included in the schedule.

Don't assume that a contractor will automatically handle every approval. Make the responsibility clear in writing, and verify project-specific requirements with the relevant local building department when needed. A builder should be able to explain how required inspections fit into the work plan.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

  • Choosing only by the lowest total without checking what each estimate includes.
  • Accepting a verbal promise instead of getting scope, materials, exclusions, and changes in writing.
  • Paying without understanding the payment milestones and how they relate to completed work.
  • Assuming the builder handles permits, cleanup, or subcontractor coordination without confirming it.
  • Making material selections late and expecting the original timeline to stay unchanged.
  • Starting work before you understand how unexpected conditions and added costs will be approved.

A clear contract can help set expectations before work starts. Read it carefully, ask for plain explanations of anything unclear, and keep approved changes and important project updates in writing.

When should you call a professional builder?

Bring in a builder when a project changes the structure, involves several trades, needs permits or inspections, or requires coordination between existing and new construction. It's also sensible to get professional input when you find damage or conditions that are not covered by the original plan.

If you're unsure whether your project needs a builder, describe the work and ask what kind of assessment is appropriate. A qualified provider should be willing to explain the limits of an initial estimate and identify what needs further review.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Can you walk me through the written scope, including exclusions and material allowances?
  • Who will manage the project day to day, and who will perform each part of the work?
  • What similar projects have you completed, and can I speak with a recent customer?
  • What permits or inspections may apply, and who is responsible for arranging them?
  • What is the estimated start window and duration, and how will schedule updates work?
  • How are unexpected conditions, added work, and change orders priced and approved?
  • What payment milestones do you use, and what warranty or written workmanship terms apply?
  • What do I need to provide or decide before work starts?

Listen for specific answers, not just broad assurances. If two providers explain the same issue differently, ask each to put their assumptions and responsibilities in the proposal so you can compare them fairly.

What is the next step?

Write down your goals, must-have features, and preferred timing, then request a site visit and a written proposal. Compare scope, exclusions, payment terms, communication, and schedule alongside the total cost.
